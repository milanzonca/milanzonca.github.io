##### Abstract

The article examines the study of philosophical works, most notably Maimonides's _Guide of the Perplexed_ and its commentaries, among the Jews living in Prague at the turn of the fifteenth century. It focuses on a Jewish author named Menaḥem ben Jacob Shalem and his fragmentary treatise discussing motifs from Maimonides’s _Guide of the Perplexed_, III:51. The article also discusses later dissemination of Menaḥem's texts.
